Simple Precision Study

CLSI EP05-A3: Simple Precision Study
Why we do this: To ensure the method produces consistent, reliable results when the same sample is measured repeatedly under specified conditions (repeatability and within-lab precision).
Step-by-Step Instructions:
  1. Select two or three levels of control material (e.g., Level 1: Normal, Level 2: Abnormal, Level 3: High/Pathological).
  2. Run each level at least 20 times (e.g., 2 runs/day for 10 days) as per your lab's policy.
  3. Enter the Date and Level results into the table below.
  4. Enter the acceptable %CV limits for each level (based on manufacturer claims or biological variation).
  5. Click "Force Calculate" to evaluate if the calculated %CV is within acceptable limits.
Formulas: Mean = Σx/n | SD = (Σ(x-x̄)²/(n-1)) | %CV = (SD/Mean)×100

Two Instrument Comparison (Qualitative)

CLSI EP12-A2: Two Instrument Comparison (Qualitative)
Why we do this: To verify that a new qualitative method agrees with the established reference method in correctly classifying samples as Reactive (Positive) or Non-Reactive (Negative), minimizing false results.
Step-by-Step Instructions:
  1. Select at least 20-40 patient samples, ensuring a mix of Reactive and Non-Reactive results (include weak positives near the cutoff).
  2. Test each sample with both the Reference Method (A) and the new Test Method (B).
  3. Enter the Sample ID and select the results (REACTIVE / NON-REACTIVE) for both methods in the table.
  4. The system will automatically flag any discrepant results in red.
  5. Click "Force Calculate" to determine Sensitivity, Specificity, and overall agreement (target ≥95%).
Formulas: Sensitivity = TP/(TP+FN)×100 | Specificity = TN/(TN+FP)×100

Two Instrument Comparison (Quantitative)

CLSI EP09-A3: Two Instrument Comparison (Quantitative)
Why we do this: To estimate the systematic bias between a new test method and a reference/comparator method, ensuring the new method's numerical results are clinically equivalent and safe for patient care.
Step-by-Step Instructions:
  1. Select at least 20-40 patient samples covering the entire reportable range (low, normal, and high concentrations).
  2. Analyze each sample on both the Reference Method (A) and the Test Method (B) within 2 hours to avoid sample stability issues.
  3. Enter the Sample ID and the numerical results for both methods in the table.
  4. Define the Total Allowable Error (TEa %) based on CLIA, RiliBÄK, or biological variation guidelines.
  5. Click "Force Calculate" to evaluate %Bias per sample and regression statistics. (Acceptable if ≤25% of samples exceed TEa, or per local policy).
Formulas: %Bias = |(B-A)/A|×100 | Regression: y = mx + b

Two Instrument Correlation Study

CLSI EP09-A3: Two Instrument Correlation Study
Why we do this: To establish the strength of the linear relationship between two quantitative methods. A high correlation indicates the methods track each other well across the entire measuring range.
Step-by-Step Instructions:
  1. Use the same dataset generated for the Quantitative Comparison (20-40 samples across the measuring range).
  2. Ensure the Sample IDs and results for Method A and Method B are accurately entered.
  3. Click "Force Calculate" to generate the Ordinary Least Squares regression equation (y = mx + b), Correlation Coefficient (r), and R².
  4. Verify that the correlation coefficient (r) is ≥ 0.95 (or 0.975), the slope is close to 1.0, and the intercept is close to 0.
Formulas: r = |Σ((x-x̄)(y-ȳ)) / √(Σ(x-x)² Σ(y-ȳ)²)|

Cut Off Verification Report

CLSI EP12-A2: Cut Off Verification Report
Why we do this: To confirm that the assay correctly classifies samples near the decision threshold (cut-off value). This is critical for qualitative or semi-quantitative tests to avoid false positives or false negatives in clinical decision-making.
Step-by-Step Instructions:
  1. Obtain samples with concentrations approximately 20% below and 20% above the manufacturer's stated cut-off value.
  2. Run at least 5 replicates for the "Lower" sample and 5 replicates for the "Higher" sample.
  3. Enter the official Cut Off Value and the Acceptance Criteria (e.g., 95% or 100% agreement).
  4. Enter the measured results for each replicate in the respective Sample 1 and Sample 2 tables.
  5. Click "Force Calculate" to verify if the percentage of correctly classified results meets the acceptance criteria.

Linearity Verification Report

CLSI EP06-A: Linearity Verification Report
Why we do this: To verify that the method provides results that are directly proportional to the concentration of the analyte in the sample across the entire claimed reportable range.
Step-by-Step Instructions:
  1. Prepare at least 4-5 samples with known target concentrations spanning the reportable range (e.g., Low, Mid-Low, Mid, Mid-High, High).
  2. Run each sample in duplicate (Run 1 and Run 2) in a single analytical run.
  3. Enter the Target concentration and the two run results for each sample level in the table.
  4. Enter the TEa (%) limit allowed for linearity evaluation.
  5. Click "Force Calculate". The system will plot the regression line and calculate the % Deviation. The method is linear if the maximum % Deviation is ≤ TEa.
Formulas: Expected = (Slope × Target) + Intercept | %Dev = |(Mean - Exp) / Target| × 100

Carry Over Verification Report

CLSI EP10-A3: Carry Over Verification Report
Why we do this: To evaluate potential contamination of subsequent samples by residual material from a previous high-concentration sample, which could cause false elevations in low-concentration samples.
Step-by-Step Instructions:
  1. Follow the standard CLSI sequence: 3 Low samples (Baseline), followed by 3 High samples, then 10 Low samples (Post-High), interspersed with High samples as defined in the pre-loaded sequence.
  2. Enter the known Low Target and High Target concentrations.
  3. Enter the Accept Criteria (%) for carryover (e.g., ≤ 1.0%).
  4. Enter the measured results for each step in the sequence exactly as they appear on the analyzer.
  5. Click "Force Calculate". The system calculates the carryover percentage. It passes if the calculated carryover is ≤ the accept criteria.
Formulas: Carryover (%) = |(Mean Post-High - Mean Baseline) / (High Mean - Baseline Mean)| × 100

Reference Intervals Verification

CLSI EP28-A3c: Reference Intervals Verification & Establishment
Why we do this: To verify that the established or manufacturer-provided reference intervals are appropriate for the laboratory's patient population, or to establish a new reference interval from a reference individual group.
Step-by-Step Instructions:
  1. Enter the proposed Lower Limit and Upper Limit for the reference interval (if verifying an existing interval).
  2. Alternatively, enter the results of at least 20 healthy reference individuals and use the "Apply Parametric Limits" button to calculate the interval using the Standard Statistical Method.
  3. Standard Statistical Method: If the data follows a normal (Gaussian) distribution, the range is calculated as the Mean ± 2 Standard Deviations (SD), which encompasses approximately 95% of the population.
  4. Click "Force Calculate". The system will determine how many results fall outside the specified interval.
  5. Acceptance Criteria (Verification): For 20 samples, if ≤ 2 results (10%) fall outside the interval, the reference interval is verified.
Formulas: Parametric: Mean ± 2 SD | Non-Parametric: 2.5th and 97.5th Percentiles | Verification: Outliers ≤ 2 (for n=20)
